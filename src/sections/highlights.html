Science & human moments

The details that stay with us.

Beyond the distance record, the mission brought new observations and moments that made the scale of the journey feel personal.

A photographer’s perspective

Look along the terminator.

Low sunlight draws long shadows across the Moon’s surface. For an astrophotographer, that boundary between day and night is familiar territory. From Orion, the crew could describe the texture, colour and shape of terrain from a different angle.

An eclipse and six flashes

NASA reported six meteoroid impact flashes on the Moon’s dark surface. The almost hour-long solar eclipse also gave the crew a view of the solar corona.

Integrity and Carroll

During the flyby, the crew proposed names for two craters: Integrity, after their spacecraft, and Carroll, in memory of commander Reid Wiseman’s late wife. These were crew proposals, rather than an announcement of official IAU names.

The everyday systems matter too

Orion’s waste system needed troubleshooting during the flight. NASA’s initial post-flight assessment identified the urine vent line for investigation and corrective work before Artemis III.
