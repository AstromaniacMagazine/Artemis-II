The name

A name built on trust

The crew announced the name Integrity on 24 September 2025. It reflects the trust placed in one another and in the people who built, tested and operated their spacecraft.

Flight history

Test flights, then a crew

Orion flew the uncrewed Exploration Flight Test-1 in 2014 and travelled around the Moon on Artemis I in 2022. Artemis II added the crucial experience of operating the spacecraft with people aboard.

Crew module

A working home in deep space

The pressurised crew module is where astronauts live, work and control Orion. Designed to support four people for up to 21 days, it brings together life support, controls, exercise equipment and essential supplies.

Heat shield

The hottest part of the journey

Orion’s ablative heat shield uses 186 Avcoat blocks to carry heat away during atmospheric entry. Its approximately 5-metre diameter protects the capsule during a return at around 25,000 mph. NASA’s initial Artemis II inspections found far less char loss than on Artemis I; that early assessment is not a final engineering certification.

Service module

Power, propulsion, air and water

The European Service Module supplies power, propulsion and life-support resources. Its 33 engines steer and propel Orion, while four solar-array wings supply electrical power. The arrays span about 19 metres when deployed.

Avionics

Computers built for space

Redundant flight computers and radiation-tolerant electronics help Orion navigate, monitor its systems and carry out flight commands. The crew can take manual control as well as use automated flight modes.

What the first inspections found

In its 20 April assessment, NASA reported that thermal protection performed as expected and heat-shield char loss was significantly reduced. The agency also identified the urine vent line for further investigation. A successful return produced evidence to study, including systems that still needed work.
