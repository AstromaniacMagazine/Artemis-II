Global partners

A shared journey, built across borders.

NASA led the mission. Canada contributed a crew member and a wider Artemis partnership. Europe supplied the service module that powered and sustained Orion.

United States / NASA

Leading the flight

NASA brought together SLS, Orion, launch operations, flight control and recovery. The flight drew on teams at Kennedy, Johnson, Marshall and across the agency and industry.

Canada / CSA

Canada around the Moon

Jeremy Hansen’s flight made Canada the second country to send one of its citizens around the Moon. His place on the crew is part of Canada’s broader contribution to Artemis.

Europe / ESA

Orion’s European powerhouse

ESA’s European Service Module provided propulsion, electricity, thermal control, water, oxygen and nitrogen. Built by European industry, it carried the systems Orion needed beyond Earth orbit.

Artemis Accords

Co-operation beyond one mission.

The Accords set out principles for peaceful, transparent and responsible exploration. They began in 2020 with eight founding signatories. San Marino became the {{facts.accords}}th signatory on 25 September 2026.

Signing the Accords does not mean a country supplied hardware for Artemis II.

The principles and current signatories ↗
Peaceful exploration and transparency

The signatories commit to peaceful activities and to sharing information about their space policies and exploration plans.

Compatible systems and emergency assistance

The principles encourage systems that can work together and assistance for astronauts in distress.

Science, heritage and space debris

The Accords support sharing scientific results, protecting historic sites and planning for debris reduction and safe operations.
